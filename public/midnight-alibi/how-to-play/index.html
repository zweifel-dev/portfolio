How to play

Every case is a small logic puzzle with a murder at its center. This page covers everything from your first Easy case to Expert.

  1. 01

    The timeline

    The Board is a grid. Each row is a suspect, each column is an hour, and each square is the room that suspect was in.

    Like a Sudoku, every suspect visits every room exactly once, and no two suspects share a room at the same hour. Tap a square, then pick a room.

  2. 02

    Question everyone

    The Testimony tab has one statement from each suspect. The Evidence corkboard holds what forensics found, and evidence is always true.

    Tap a statement to cross it off once you have used it. On the corkboard, tap one pin and then another to run red string between clues.

  3. 03

    Name the killer

    The body was found in one room at the time of death. Whoever was in that room then is the killer. As soon as someone is placed at the scene at that hour, the Accuse button appears, so you don’t have to finish the whole board. Be sure: accuse the wrong suspect and the case earns no rank points.

    On Hard and Expert, the killer’s testimony is a lie. Find the statement that can’t be true.

When you’re stuck

Help that teaches

Hint

A nudge comes first. It names the method and points at the right square. Tap Show answer to see the answer, with every step explained. Each answer costs the case a rank point, though a solved case always earns at least one.

Check your work

If a square you placed doesn’t fit the clues, the hint outlines it in red. Show answer clears it so you can work it out again.

Hunch

Pencil in the rooms a square might be: tap a room once for a maybe, twice to rule it out, and a third time to clear it. When you place a room, it is removed from the maybes in the rest of that row and hour.

The methods

The eleven methods

Every case can be solved by logic alone, with exactly one answer. These are the methods in order, from the first you will need to the hardest.

  • Easy and up

    A clue says so

    A clue says exactly where someone was at a given hour.

  • Easy and up

    Only one place left

    Scan a row or an hour. Every square but one is blocked because that suspect already visited the room, or someone else is already in it at that hour.

  • Easy and up

    Rule it out

    Rule a room out of a square because putting it there would contradict a clue.

  • Easy and up

    Only one room left

    Every room but one has been ruled out for a square, so it must be that room.

  • Easy and up

    Only one fit

    A room can fit in only one square of a suspect’s row or of an hour, once the ruled-out squares are taken into account.

  • Hard and up

    Catch the liar

    A testimony contradicts the board. Only the killer lies, so its speaker is the killer, which places them at the crime scene.

  • Medium and up

    Two rooms, two squares

    Two squares in the same row or hour can each only be the same two rooms. Those two rooms are used up, so rule them out everywhere else in that line.

  • Medium and up

    A pair hides in the row

    Two rooms can only go in the same two squares of a row or hour. Those squares must hold those rooms, so rule out every other room there.

  • Hard and up

    Cross-check two rows

    In two suspects’ rows, a room can only go at the same two hours. One suspect takes each hour, so nobody else was in that room at those hours. It also works with hours and suspects swapped.

  • Hard and up

    Chain of three

    A square that can only be A or B shares a line with one square that is A or C and another that is B or C. Whichever way the first goes, one of the other two is C, so any square in line with both can’t be C.

  • Expert and up

    Cross-check three rows

    Like cross-checking two rows, but with three: in three suspects’ rows, a room is confined to the same three hours, so nobody else was in that room at those hours. It also works with hours and suspects swapped.
